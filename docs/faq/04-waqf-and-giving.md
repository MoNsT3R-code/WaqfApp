# Waqf, Sadaqah and Islamic Giving

## Is the religious guidance in this FAQ a fatwa?

No. This section explains common Islamic concepts in general terms to help you understand the ideas behind WaqfApp. It is not a religious ruling (fatwa) and does not replace the advice of a qualified scholar. Schools of Islamic jurisprudence differ on many details, such as the minimum threshold for Zakat or how a Waqf may be managed. For any decision that matters to you, particularly regarding Zakat, inheritance, loans or establishing a Waqf, please consult a trusted scholar or a recognised Shariah advisory body.

## What is a Waqf?

A Waqf is a charitable endowment in Islamic law. The founder (the waqif) permanently dedicates an asset, such as land, a building, a well, books or a fund of money, so that the asset itself is preserved while its benefit or income is used for a charitable purpose that the founder specifies, such as feeding the poor, funding a school or maintaining a mosque. The asset can no longer be sold, gifted or inherited. A manager, called the mutawalli or nazir, looks after the Waqf according to the founder's conditions. The word is written وقف in Arabic and Urdu (الوقف, al-waqf, in Arabic with the article).

## What is the origin of the Waqf tradition?

The best-known foundation is a narration in the collections of al-Bukhari and Muslim. Umar ibn al-Khattab acquired valuable land at Khaybar and asked the Prophet Muhammad, peace be upon him, what to do with it. The Prophet advised him that he could keep the land itself and give its produce in charity. Umar did so, on the condition that the land would not be sold, gifted or inherited and that its yield would go to the poor, relatives, travellers and other good causes. This became the model for awqaf throughout Islamic history.

## What is Sadaqah Jariyah?

Sadaqah Jariyah means "continuous charity": giving whose benefit continues after the act of giving, and even after the giver's death. A well-known narration in Sahih Muslim states that when a person dies their deeds come to an end except for three: a continuing charity, knowledge from which benefit is derived, and a righteous child who prays for them. The WaqfApp home screen shows this hadith. In Urdu it is written صدقہ جاریہ and in Arabic صدقة جارية. Examples of Sadaqah Jariyah include building a well, planting a tree, endowing a school, distributing copies of the Quran, or contributing to a Waqf.

## What is the verse shown on the WaqfApp home screen?

The home screen shows a summary of the meaning of Surah Al-Baqarah, verse 261, which compares spending in the way of Allah to a single grain that grows seven ears, each bearing a hundred grains, and says that Allah multiplies the reward for whom He wills. The app phrases it simply: helping others is like planting a seed that grows into many more. The verse is often quoted to encourage generous and sincere giving.

## What is the difference between Zakat and Sadaqah?

Zakat is an obligatory annual payment, one of the five pillars of Islam, due from a Muslim whose wealth is above a minimum threshold (nisab) and has been held for a full lunar year. It is calculated at a fixed rate on eligible wealth and may be given only to the categories of recipients named in the Quran (Surah At-Tawbah, verse 60). In Urdu these are زکوٰۃ and صدقہ, and in Arabic الزكاة and الصدقة. Sadaqah is voluntary charity: it can be given at any time, in any amount, to anyone in need, and can include non-monetary kindness such as a smile or helping someone carry their load.

## How is Zakat usually calculated?

In broad terms, Zakat is 2.5 percent of qualifying wealth held for one lunar year above the nisab. Qualifying wealth typically includes cash, bank balances, gold and silver, business stock and certain investments, minus some immediate debts. The nisab is commonly set at the value of 87.48 grams of gold or 612.36 grams of silver; many scholars recommend using the silver value because it is lower and so benefits more of the poor. Exact rules vary between schools of thought, so use a reliable Zakat calculator or ask a scholar.

## Who is eligible to receive Zakat?

Surah At-Tawbah, verse 60, names eight categories: the poor (fuqara), the needy (masakin), those employed to collect and administer Zakat, those whose hearts are to be reconciled, freeing captives, those in debt, in the way of Allah, and the wayfarer (a traveller in need). Most scholars hold that Zakat cannot be given to one's own parents, grandparents, children or spouse, because supporting them is already one's duty, though other relatives in need are often considered among the most deserving recipients.

## How can a digital wallet support Sadaqah Jariyah?

A digital wallet makes it easy to give small amounts regularly, which the Prophet, peace be upon him, praised: the most beloved deeds to Allah are those done consistently, even if they are small. A wallet can help by reminding you to give, by allowing recurring contributions to an endowment fund, by keeping a record of your giving for Zakat and budgeting, and by making it simple to support projects with lasting benefit such as wells, schools and clinics.

## Can a Waqf be made with cash rather than land?

Yes, according to many contemporary scholars and historical practice, particularly in the Ottoman era. In a cash Waqf, the endowed money is preserved and invested in Shariah-compliant ways, and the profits are spent on the charitable purpose. Modern cash Waqf funds and Waqf-based investment certificates operate on this principle. Some classical scholars held reservations about endowing cash because it can be consumed, so it is worth asking how any particular cash Waqf scheme preserves the capital.

## Who manages a Waqf?

A Waqf is managed by a mutawalli (trustee or administrator) appointed by the founder or, if none is appointed, by a court or the relevant authority. The mutawalli must look after the endowed asset, collect its income and spend it according to the founder's conditions, and is accountable for doing so. In Pakistan, provincial Auqaf departments administer many endowed properties, such as shrines and mosques, under provincial Waqf laws.

## Can I make a Waqf for my family?

Yes. Islamic law recognises both a charitable Waqf (waqf khayri), dedicated to public good, and a family Waqf (waqf ahli or dhurri), whose benefit goes first to the founder's descendants and eventually to a charitable purpose when the family line ends. The legal treatment of family Waqfs varies between countries, so if you are thinking of creating one, seek advice from both a scholar and a lawyer in your jurisdiction.

## What is riba and why does it matter for financial apps?

Riba is usually translated as interest or usury: a predetermined increase over the principal of a loan, or certain unequal exchanges of the same type of goods. The Quran prohibits riba strongly (for example Surah Al-Baqarah, verses 275 to 279), and Islamic finance is designed to avoid it. For a financial app this matters for loans, savings accounts and investments: a Shariah-conscious user will want to know whether returns or charges are based on interest or on permitted structures such as profit-sharing, leasing or trade.

## What Shariah-compliant alternatives to interest-based loans exist?

Common alternatives include Qard al-Hasan, a benevolent loan repaid without any increase; Murabaha, in which a financier buys an item and sells it to you at a disclosed mark-up, payable in instalments; Ijarah, a lease; Musharakah and Diminishing Musharakah, partnership arrangements often used for home finance; and Mudarabah, in which one party provides capital and the other expertise and profits are shared. Pakistan has a substantial Islamic banking sector regulated by the State Bank of Pakistan that offers these products.

## Is it permissible to use a digital wallet at all?

Using a digital wallet to hold money and make payments is generally considered permissible, much like holding cash or using a current account, provided that the wallet is not used for prohibited transactions and that you are not earning interest on the balance. Questions sometimes arise about wallets that pay interest-like rewards on balances or that promote interest-based credit; if this concerns you, choose products that are Shariah-compliant and ask a scholar.

## What does "Bismillah" at the top of the app mean?

"Bismillah ir-Rahman ir-Rahim" means "In the name of Allah, the Most Gracious, the Most Merciful". Muslims say it before beginning actions to seek blessing and to remember that their work should be done for good purposes. It opens every chapter of the Quran except one. WaqfApp displays it at the top of the home screen as a reminder that managing money is also a matter of trust (amanah) and intention.

## Why does WaqfApp emphasise intention in spending?

In Islamic teaching, actions are judged by their intentions, as in the famous hadith that opens Sahih al-Bukhari. Wealth is seen as a trust from Allah, and a Muslim is accountable for how it is earned and spent. Avoiding waste (israf), spending on one's family, which is itself rewarded, and setting aside a share for others are all encouraged. WaqfApp's design, from the Money Helper to the Portfolio, aims to make spending more deliberate so that giving becomes easier.

## How much of my income should I give in charity?

Beyond the obligatory Zakat, there is no fixed amount for voluntary charity. The Quran encourages spending "what is beyond your needs" (Surah Al-Baqarah, verse 219), and scholars advise not giving so much that you neglect your own dependants. Many people find it helpful to commit to a fixed small percentage of every payment they receive, or a fixed monthly amount, so that giving is consistent. Even small, regular amounts can achieve a great deal over time.

## Is it better to give charity openly or secretly?

Both are praised. The Quran says that giving charity openly is good, but giving it secretly to the poor is better for you (Surah Al-Baqarah, verse 271). Giving openly can encourage others to give, while giving secretly protects sincerity and the dignity of the recipient. Many people give obligatory Zakat openly through organised channels and voluntary Sadaqah privately.

## How can I make sure my charity reaches people who need it?

Give through registered, transparent organisations that publish accounts and report on their projects, or directly to people you know to be in need. In Pakistan, check that a charity is registered with the relevant authority and, for tax purposes, approved under the income tax rules for donations. Be cautious about appeals that pressure you to give immediately, that cannot explain how funds are used, or that ask for payment to personal accounts.
